# Sources for "The story"

Gathered 2026-10-22 by research agents that quoted only pages they fetched. Tags: [evidence] a controlled study,
[practitioner] a speaker's or creator's own advice, [single source] one secondary report. Most of this is
practitioner advice; the studies use students and short recorded material, and several effects are weaker for
expert audiences.

## The seven moves

1. **Premise and claim titles**
   - Alon, "How to give a good talk" [practitioner]: https://psych.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/75/HowToGiveAGoodTalk.pdf
   - Garner & Alley 2013, assertion–evidence vs topic–bullet slides, 110 students, 8-min recorded talks, comprehension
     d = 0.81, run by the method's authors [evidence]: https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol29-6/23_ijee2791ns.pdf
   - McConnell, iBiology, "Designing effective scientific presentations" [practitioner]: https://www.ibiology.org/?p=3514
2. **Phenomenon and question first**
   - Peyton Jones, "How to give a great research talk" ("You have 2 minutes to engage your audience"; "No outline!")
     [practitioner]: https://www2.cs.ucy.ac.cy/courses/EPL670/docs/GivingATalk.pdf
   - Card, Sussex Visions seminar 2022 (opens from Lettvin's "What the frog's eye tells the frog's brain"):
     https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S36yH9zHxRw
3. **Predict before the result**
   - Crouch et al. 2004, classroom demonstrations [evidence]: https://www.compadre.org/portal/items/detail.cfm?ID=2329
   - Pretesting / prequestions [evidence, secondary; weaker for real lecture videos]:
     https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/11/2-1,
     https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/the-limited-effects-of-prequestions-on-learning-from-authentic-le/
4. **Concrete before abstract**
   - Sanderson, 3Blue1Brown FAQ ("Never start with definitions") [practitioner]: https://www.3blue1brown.com/faq/
   - Summer of Math Exposition guidance ("CONCRETE then ABSTRACT") [practitioner]:
     https://github.com/3b1b/3Blue1Brown.com/blob/main/app/pages/blog/some1/index.mdx
   - Fyfe et al. 2014, concreteness fading review [evidence]: https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-014-9249-3
5. **Name the wrong model**
   - Muller, misconceptions in physics videos [evidence]:
     https://thejournal.com/articles/2015/05/05/watching-videos-does-not-necessarily-lead-to-learning.aspx
   - Winston, MIT "How to Speak" ("build a fence") [practitioner]:
     https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-371-innovation-teams-fall-2024/Unzc731iCUY_transcript.pdf
6. **Home slide**
   - McConnell (above); Winston (above, "verbal punctuation")
   - Signaling meta-analysis, r = 0.17, mostly for low prior knowledge [evidence, indirect]:
     https://psycharchives.org/handle/20.500.12034/2018
7. **End on contributions**
   - Winston (above); McConnell (above)

## Also found, not in the table

- **Humor and digressions.** Interesting-but-irrelevant material ("seductive details") lowers learning, most at
  the start (Sundar & Adesope 2020, 58 studies) [evidence]: https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning.
  Humor related to the content helps attention and interest (Banas et al. 2011 review; Ziv 1988, 3–4 related jokes
  per lesson) [evidence, mixed]: https://www.facultyfocus.com/articles/effective-teaching-strategies/humor-in-the-classroom-40-years-of-research/,
  https://www.siop.org/tip-article/max-classroom-capacity-the-serious-business-of-humor-in-the-classroom/
- **Dickinson** (public talks only): TEDx https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OgidK7dRW0; Caltech Watson Lecture
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oZ6k9h6H6w; iBiology "How Flies Fly" https://www.ibiology.org/biophysics/how-flies-fly/;
  "My lectures tend to be pretty high energy… I tell a lot of jokes": https://www.unr.edu/nevada-today/news/2017/dickinson-feature
- **Animation.** Berney & Bétrancourt 2016 meta-analysis, 61 studies, g = 0.23 overall, larger when the presentation
  sets the pace and is narrated [evidence]: https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/75bf6876-cb91-4e2c-8ac6-9d0767449449/download;
  Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt 2002 ("Animations are often too complex or too fast to be accurately perceived")
  [evidence]: https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde411/w13/readings/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf;
  build a graph up piece by piece (Knaflic) [practitioner]: https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/06/leveraging-animation-what-you-present
